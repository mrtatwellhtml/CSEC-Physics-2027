In simple words

Fission: a slow neutron hits a big nucleus such as uranium-235, which splits into two smaller nuclei and releases 2 or 3 more neutrons plus a lot of energy. Those neutrons can split more nuclei: a chain reaction.

In a nuclear power station, control rods absorb some neutrons so that, on average, one neutron from each fission causes another, keeping the reaction steady. Fusion is the opposite: small nuclei join together (as in the Sun).

E = mc²: where does the energy come from?

The products of fission have slightly less mass than the uranium + neutron. The missing mass Δm turns into energy: E = Δm c², with c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s. Because c² is huge, a tiny mass gives a huge energy.
