In simple words

A thermometer needs two fixed points: temperatures that are always the same. The ice point (pure melting ice) is 0 °C. The steam point (steam above boiling water at normal pressure) is 100 °C.

Mark the liquid thread at both points, then divide the gap into 100 equal parts. For any other temperature, see what fraction of the way from the ice point to the steam point the thread has reached.

Celsius and kelvin

T (K) = θ (°C) + 273. 0 K (−273 °C) is absolute zero, the lowest possible temperature, where particles have the least possible kinetic energy. A change of 1 °C is the same size as a change of 1 K.
