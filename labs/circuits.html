In simple words

Current (I) is the flow of charge, measured in amps with an ammeter placed in the circuit. Voltage (potential difference, V) is the energy given to each coulomb of charge, measured with a voltmeter placed across a component. Resistance R = V ÷ I: how hard it is for current to flow.

Series (one loop): the same current flows everywhere, and the voltages share out. Parallel (branches): every branch gets the full voltage, and the currents split.

Common mistakes

Current is not used up by a lamp: the same current comes out as goes in. Ammeters go in series, voltmeters go in parallel. Adding a resistor in parallel makes the total resistance smaller, not bigger.
